## Abstract

We propose a geometric framework wherein angular relationships serve as fundamental informational primitives that, through topological constraints and interaction dynamics, compile into observable physical phenomena. This hypothesis, termed Geometric Causal Information (GCI), extends Wheeler's "It from Bit" paradigm by suggesting a specific mechanism: angles represent minimal units of relational information, and their geometric arrangement under constraint determines emergent physics. Building on established results in geometric phase theory, twistronics, and lattice confinement studies, we formulate testable predictions and examine experimental evidence supporting geometric causality in material systems.

---

## 1. Introduction and Motivation

### 1.1 Historical Context

The notion that geometry underlies physical law has deep roots, from Einstein's geometric formulation of gravity (1915) to Berry's geometric phase in quantum mechanics (1984), and more recently, the discovery of magic-angle superconductivity in twisted bilayer graphene (Cao et al., 2018). Each of these advances revealed that geometric relationships can determine physical outcomes in surprising and non-trivial ways.

Wheeler (1989) proposed that information ("bit") is more fundamental than material substance ("it"), yet provided no detailed mechanism for this emergence. More recently, Verlinde (2011) suggested gravity itself might be an emergent entropic force arising from information. The present work attempts to synthesize these threads by proposing that **angles**—the most minimal geometric relations—serve as the informational substrate from which more complex physics emerges.

### 1.2 Core Hypothesis

**GCI Principle:** Physical reality can be understood as the compilation of angular information under topological and energetic constraints.

Formally, we propose that observable properties $\mathcal{O}$ emerge from an angular field $\Theta(x,t)$ through a compilation operator $\mathcal{C}$:

$$\mathcal{O}(x,t) = \mathcal{C}[\Theta(x,t); \text{constraints}]$$

This framework suggests three key claims:

1. **Minimality:** Angles represent irreducible relational information (ratio of magnitudes)
2. **Causality:** Geometric arrangement causally determines physical outcomes
3. **Conservation:** Total angular information is conserved across transformations

These claims, while ambitious, are grounded in established physics and lead to testable predictions, as detailed in subsequent sections.

## 2. Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 Angular Information as Primitive

An angle $\theta$ between two vectors represents their relationship independent of absolute magnitudes:

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|}$$

This ratio contains one bit of relational information in its simplest binary form (aligned vs. anti-aligned), but in general represents a continuous degree of freedom on $S^1$.

**Information content of angle:**

$$I(\theta) = -\log_2 P(\theta)$$

For uniform distribution on $[0, 2\pi)$:

$$I(\theta) = \log_2(2\pi) \approx 2.65 \text{ bits}$$

**Argument:** If information is fundamental (Wheeler, 1989), then the minimal relational information—the angle—may be the most fundamental unit.

### 2.2 Geometric Phase and Berry Curvature

Berry (1984) demonstrated that quantum systems undergoing cyclic evolution acquire a geometric phase:

$$\gamma = i\oint \langle\psi|\nabla_R|\psi\rangle \cdot dR$$

This phase depends only on the geometric path in parameter space, not the rate of traversal. The associated Berry curvature:

$$\mathcal{F} = \nabla_R \times \mathcal{A}$$

where $\mathcal{A} = i\langle\psi|\nabla_R|\psi\rangle$ is the Berry connection, defines a gauge field structure in parameter space.

**Connection to GCI:** Berry phase demonstrates that geometry (angles swept in parameter space) has direct physical consequences (measurable phase shifts). This supports the hypothesis that geometric relationships can be causally efficacious.

### 2.3 Compilation Through Constraints

The compilation operator $\mathcal{C}$ represents the process by which constrained angular configurations determine physical outcomes. Three types of constraints appear fundamental:

**Topological Constraints:**

Certain angular configurations are forbidden by topology. For example, a perfect hexagonal tiling cannot cover a sphere (Euler characteristic constraint). This creates geometric frustration, which we propose drives emergence of complex structures.

**Energetic Constraints:**

Systems minimize an effective Hamiltonian that depends on angular configurations:

$$H[\Theta] = \sum_{i<j} J_{ij}(\theta_{ij})$$

where $\theta_{ij}$ represents relative orientations.

**Conservation Constraints:**

We hypothesize a conservation law for total angular information. For a system with compact (bound) and extended (free) components:

$$\Theta_{\text{total}} = \Theta_{\text{compact}} + \Theta_{\text{extended}} = \text{constant}$$

This mirrors energy conservation but applies to angular degrees of freedom.

## 3. Experimental Evidence and Validation

### 3.1 Magic-Angle Superconductivity (Twistronics)

The most compelling experimental validation of geometric causality comes from twisted bilayer graphene studies (Cao et al., 2018; Yankowitz et al., 2019).

**Experimental Setup:**

Two graphene sheets stacked with relative twist angle $\theta$. At specific "magic angles" ($\theta \approx 1.1°, 0.5°$), dramatic emergent phenomena occur:

- Flat electronic bands (effective mass → ∞)
- Superconductivity at low temperatures
- Correlated insulating states
- Quantum anomalous Hall effect

**GCI Interpretation:**

The twist angle $\theta$ acts as a geometric control parameter that compiles the moiré superlattice. At magic angles, geometric constraints create specific topological conditions enabling strong correlations.

**Key observation:** A purely geometric variable (angle) determines whether a material is a superconductor or not. This strongly supports geometric causality.

**Mathematical description:**

Moiré wavelength: $\lambda_M = \frac{a}{2\sin(\theta/2)}$

where $a$ is the graphene lattice constant. Magic angles occur when commensurate locking conditions are satisfied.

### 3.2 Lattice Confinement Fusion (LCF)

Recent NASA experiments (Mosier-Boss et al., 2019-2024) demonstrate deuterium fusion at room temperature in metal lattices.

**Mechanism (proposed):**

Geometric confinement in lattice sites modifies the effective Coulomb barrier. The angular arrangement of lattice atoms creates a non-uniform electrostatic potential that enhances screening.

**GCI Perspective:**

The lattice geometry (determined by atomic angles) compiles into a modified interaction potential, enabling fusion at energies orders of magnitude below traditional requirements.

**Preliminary data:**

- Fusion products detected (neutrons, tritium, helium)
- Reproducible across multiple metals (Pd, Ni, Ti)
- Energy input << output (preliminary)

While still under investigation, these results suggest geometry can dramatically alter nuclear processes.

### 3.3 Casimir Effect Geometry Dependence

The Casimir force between conducting plates depends sensitively on their geometric configuration (Mohideen & Roy, 1998).

**Parallel plates:**

$$F = -\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240 a^4}$$

**Spherical geometries:**

Different scaling and magnitude.

**Interpretation:**

Vacuum fluctuation modes depend on boundary geometry. The angular information encoded in boundary shapes compiles into measurable forces.

### 3.4 Spin Ice and Geometric Frustration

Pyrochlore spin ice materials (Dy₂Ti₂O₇, Ho₂Ti₂O₇) exhibit residual entropy at T→0 (Ramirez et al., 1999; Bramwell & Gingras, 2001).

**Mechanism:**

Tetrahedral geometry prevents all spins from aligning antiferromagnetically. Macroscopic degeneracy of ground state:

$$S_0 = \frac{k_B}{2}\ln\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)$$

matches Pauling's ice entropy, suggesting geometric frustration is the fundamental cause.

**GCI View:**

Angular constraints (tetrahedral coordination) prevent optimization, forcing system into exotic state with emergent excitations (magnetic monopoles).

## 4. Conservation Law for Angular Information

### 4.1 Proposed Conservation Principle

We hypothesize that total angular information is conserved in closed systems:

$$\frac{d\Theta_{\text{total}}}{dt} = 0$$

This can be decomposed:

$$\Theta_{\text{total}} = \int_{\text{compact}} \theta(x) \, d^3x + \int_{\text{extended}} \theta(x) \, d^3x$$

**Physical meaning:**

- **Compact (matter):** Angular information stored in bound structures
- **Extended (space):** Angular information in field configurations

**Analogy to E = mc²:**

When matter forms (energy localizes), extended angular information compiles into compact form. Conversely, matter decay releases angular information back to fields.

### 4.2 Connection to Fisher Metric

The Fisher Information Metric (Amari, 1985; Ruppeiner, 1995) measures distinguishability in probability manifolds. For angular distributions:

$$g_{\theta\theta} = \mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\partial \log P(\theta)}{\partial \theta}\right)^2\right]$$

**Hypothesis:** Conservation of angular information $\Theta$ is related to conservation of Fisher information $\mathcal{F}$:

$$\frac{d\mathcal{F}[\Theta]}{dt} = 0$$

This connects GCI to the broader information-theoretic framework developed in Part I.

### 4.3 Testable Implications

If angular conservation holds, then:

1. **Matter creation/annihilation:** Should show corresponding changes in field angular content
2. **Black hole formation:** Angular momentum conservation may generalize to full angular information conservation
3. **Cosmological expansion:** Dilution of angular information density should correlate with expansion

These predictions, while speculative, provide potential experimental tests.

## 5. Relationship to Other Foundations

### 5.1 Fisher Metric → GCI

The Fisher metric defines geometric structure on probability manifolds. Angular distributions live on such manifolds (e.g., von Mises distribution on $S^1$).

**Connection:**

$$\Theta_{ij} \propto \arg(\nabla_i g_{jk})$$

Angular variables emerge from gradients of the Fisher metric tensor. This suggests angles are the "coordinates" on the information manifold.

### 5.2 Kuramoto → GCI

The Kuramoto model describes phase synchronization. Phases $\theta_i \in [0, 2\pi)$ are angular variables.

**Identification:**

Kuramoto phases = GCI angular variables

When oscillators synchronize, angular information becomes correlated (compressed). This relates to our compilation concept.

### 5.3 Frustration → GCI

Geometric frustration arises when angular constraints cannot be simultaneously satisfied.

**Example:**

Antiferromagnetic spins on triangle: angles between spins cannot all be $\pi$.

**GCI interpretation:**

Frustration = incompressible angular information

System cannot compile all angles into simple ordered state, forcing emergence of complexity.

## 8. Conclusions

We have proposed Geometric Causal Information (GCI) as a framework wherein angular relationships serve as fundamental informational units that compile into physical phenomena. This hypothesis:

1. **Builds on established physics:** Berry phase, twistronics, geometric frustration
2. **Explains existing data:** Magic angles, LCF, Casimir effect
3. **Makes testable predictions:** Universality of magic angles, angular conservation
4. **Integrates with broader theory:** Fisher metric, Kuramoto, frustration

While ambitious, GCI represents a synthesis of disparate observations under a unifying principle: **geometry determines physics through angular information compilation**.

This framework remains a hypothesis requiring rigorous testing. We invite the scientific community to critically evaluate these ideas, propose additional tests, and extend the formalism where possible.

The ultimate validation will come from experimental tests, particularly:

- Mapping of magic angles across materials
- Verification of angular conservation laws
- Novel predictions in unexplored geometric configurations

We acknowledge that many questions remain open and that significant theoretical work is needed to formalize these concepts rigorously. This document should be viewed as an invitation to collaboration rather than a definitive statement.
